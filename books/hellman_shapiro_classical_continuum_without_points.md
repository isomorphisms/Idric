# Hellman and Shapiro — The Classical Continuum without Points

**Paper:** Geoffrey Hellman and Stewart Shapiro, *The Review of Symbolic Logic*
6(3), 2013, pp. 488–512.
**DOI:** [10.1017/S1755020313000075](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1755020313000075).
**Scope:** published abstract; selected construction and reference passages in
an [author-uploaded manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259435714_The_Classical_Continuum_without_Points).
This is not a line-by-line proof audit. Manuscript and journal pagination differ.

## Source notes

The construction starts with regions and a part relation, not point-membership.
Atomlessness means that every region has a proper part. Additional geometric
axioms give a line; atomlessness alone does not determine one.

The authors retain classical logic and actual infinity. Their interval
structure yields the Archimedean property, an analogue of Dedekind completeness,
and a reconstruction of classical real-line structure. Point-free ontology
therefore need not mean different classical arithmetic.

Their gunky line admits exhaustive disjoint decomposition. Being made of no
point-parts and being indecomposable are different requirements.

In the manuscript, footnote 6 and reference [10] credit Lewis's *Parts of
Classes* (Blackwell, 1991) for the term “gunk.” This locates the Lewis connection;
it does not attribute Hellman and Shapiro's construction to Lewis.

**Correction record:** the journal lists a
[2013 corrigendum, p. 571](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1755020313000117).
Its contents have not been checked here; consult it before formalizing the proofs.

## Connection to Idriç — our interpretation

A structure-preserving translation is not permission to discard the source
concepts. An implementation might calculate using coordinates while the
language continues to describe regions, parts, and their geometric relations.

Likewise, recovering a point structure does not require declaring points to
have been primitive all along. Keep the reconstruction and its assumptions
inspectable instead of replacing the original theory with its convenient model.

This matters to the current numerical discussion: equal calculated answers do
not by themselves establish equal conceptual derivations. It does not establish
our particular negative-divisor rule; that rule remains an Idriç design choice.
