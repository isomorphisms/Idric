# Predrag Cvitanović — birdtracks

## Primary source

Predrag Cvitanović, *Group Theory: Birdtracks, Lie's, and Exceptional Groups*, Princeton University Press, 2008. ISBN 978-0-691-11836-9.

Canonical public material:

- webbook: https://birdtracks.eu/
- stable book PDF served by the author: https://birdtracks.eu/version9.0/GroupTheory.pdf
- table of contents: https://birdtracks.eu/TOC.html
- related literature and historical sources: https://birdtracks.eu/refs/
- publisher/book information: https://birdtracks.eu/PUPlink.htm

The webbook is especially useful here because the diagrams are not decorative illustrations added after the algebra. They are the notation in which invariant-tensor calculations are actually carried out.

## Why this source belongs next to Einstein inference

Birdtracks give a concrete answer to the question, “what remains after the arbitrary names of contracted indices are removed?” A tensor expression can be viewed as typed pieces with legs, with contraction represented by connection. Free legs remain at the boundary. The resulting object is closer to the mathematical dependency/wiring structure than a long coordinate expression with disposable index letters.

That is directly relevant to the present experiment:

```text
ωᵢ vⁱ
```

should not have to remain a string containing two matching glyphs. After parsing and type checking, it can become a typed connection between a lower `i` occurrence and an upper `i` occurrence, with the index name serving as surface binding syntax rather than permanent machine representation.

Cvitanović also uses diagrammatic projection operators, symmetrizers/antisymmetrizers, recouplings, group integrals, and invariant tensors as calculational objects. Those are useful precedents for representing mathematical operations structurally before deciding how to evaluate them numerically.
